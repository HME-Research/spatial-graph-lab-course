Hand in your work

A toolkit is handed in by pushing your branch: your tutor sees it on GitHub, and GitHub checks it for you. Work done only in the browser is handed in as a project file.

A toolkit: push your branch

  1. Push 
    git push -u origin toolkit/<toolkit-id>
    (later pushes: just git push; GitHub Desktop: Publish branch / Push origin). No pull request is needed: your tutor collects the branches.
  2. Find it on GitHubOpen the course repository's branches and click toolkit/<toolkit-id>.

    your latest commit message at the top, and your folder in toolkits/.

  3. Wait for the checkBeside your latest commit, a yellow dot turns into a green ✓ or a red ✗ after a few minutes. Click it to see the checks:
    • Typecheck, tests and build: the whole repository still works with your branch.
    • Student branch changes only its toolkit: nothing outside toolkits/<toolkit-id>/ changed, and your toolkit loads and runs (check:toolkit).
  4. Red ✗?Click Details next to the failing check and read the red lines.
    The message saysDo this
    This branch changes files outside toolkits/…Put those files back (Git › I changed a file outside my folder), commit, push.
    the toolkit id must be lowercase…Your branch name is wrong. Ask your tutor.
    a check:toolkit or typecheck errorRun the same check on your computer, fix it inside your folder (paste the message to your AI), commit, push.

Your tutor can open your pushed branch in the app online, with your toolkit in it. Keep your README.md up to date: it is the first thing they read. Say what the toolkit is for, its Functions, attributes, algorithms and designs, and how to try it.

An analysis, transform or design made in the browser

In the app: Project › Export project, and hand in the .project.json file the way your tutor asks. It holds your design, its scenarios and the analyses and transforms you uploaded (In the browser).
